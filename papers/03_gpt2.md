# Language Models are Unsupervised Multitask Learners / GPT-2 (Radford et al., 2019)

## Gist

Train a big decoder-only Transformer on one dumb task — predict the next
word on 40 GB of web text — and it starts doing translation, summarization,
and QA with **zero task-specific training**. The paper's real claim: scale
plus a general objective produces multitask behavior for free. OpenAI
considered GPT-2 dangerous enough to withhold at first, which tells you how
surprising zero-shot behavior was in 2019.

## How it works

A 1.5B-parameter decoder-only Transformer (moved layer norms, 1024-token
context, byte-level BPE so nothing is ever out-of-vocabulary) trains purely
on next-token prediction over **WebText** — pages linked from Reddit with
karma filtering as a human-quality heuristic. At test time there is no
finetuning at all: the task is described in prose ("translate to French:…")
and the model continues the pattern. Sampling uses temperature and top-k to
trade coherence against diversity. Every "emergent" skill is just the same
continuation machinery pointed at a new format.

## Key concepts

- **Autoregressive modeling**: factor text as P(next word | all previous
  words). One objective, endless emergent skills.
- **Zero-shot**: solve a task from a natural-language description alone, no
  examples, no finetuning.
- **WebText**: 40 GB of curated outbound-Reddit-linked pages — the first
  famous lesson that data curation matters as much as architecture.
- **Task conditioning**: the model infers what to do from the prompt format
  itself, the ancestor of every structured prompt.
- **Staged release**: the authors' safety framing; worth knowing because the
  same debate now surrounds every open-weight release.

## Why learn this

GPT-2 teaches the most leveraged idea in modern ML: a simple objective at
scale beats clever architectures. It also retrains your instincts — every
new task becomes "how do I frame this as continuation?" — which is the
mindset behind prompting, agents, and tool use alike.
