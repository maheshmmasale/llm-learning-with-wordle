# Language Models are Few-Shot Learners / GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020)

## Gist

GPT-2 could zero-shot; GPT-3 at 175B parameters can **few-shot**: show it a
handful of input→output examples inside the prompt and it performs new tasks
with no gradient updates at all. Bigger models are disproportionately better
in-context learners — the few-shot/zero-shot gap widens with scale. This is
the paper that made prompting a research field instead of a party trick.

## Key concepts

- **In-context learning**: the model learns the task from examples in its
  context window at inference time; weights never change.
- **Zero/one/few-shot**: zero = description only, one = one example, few =
  several. Your prompting milestone should test all three separately.
- **Emergence with scale**: few-shot ability appears and strengthens with
  size — small models like yours will be much worse at it, which is why you
  finetune instead.
- **Prompt as program**: the context window is the interface; formatting
  choices (your `GUESS:` contract) measurably change accuracy.
- **Contamination awareness**: GPT-3's authors had to check test data wasn't
  in training — the ancestor of your leakage audits.

## Why it matters here

Milestone 3 is applied GPT-3 thinking: how much Wordle skill can you unlock
from a small model with examples and structure alone, before spending any
training compute? The paper says: measure zero vs few carefully.
