# Language Models are Few-Shot Learners / GPT-3 (Brown et al., 2020)

## Gist

GPT-2 could zero-shot; GPT-3 at 175B parameters can **few-shot**: show it a
handful of input→output examples inside the prompt and it performs new tasks
with no gradient updates at all. Bigger models are disproportionately better
in-context learners — the few-shot/zero-shot gap widens with scale. This is
the paper that made prompting a research field instead of a party trick.

## How it works

Same decoder-only recipe at 175B parameters on 300B tokens, with alternating
dense and sparse attention layers for efficiency. The evaluation is the
innovation as much as the model: every benchmark is run in zero-, one-, and
few-shot settings, revealing that few-shot performance climbs faster with
size than zero-shot does. A full contamination analysis checks test data
against training data — because at web scale, memorization is the null
hypothesis you must rule out before claiming learning.

## Key concepts

- **In-context learning**: the model learns the task from examples in its
  context window at inference time; weights never change.
- **Zero/one/few-shot**: zero = description only, one = one example, few =
  several. Always test all three separately — they measure different things.
- **Emergence with scale**: few-shot ability appears and strengthens with
  size — small models are much worse at it, which is why they get finetuned.
- **Prompt as program**: the context window is the interface; formatting
  choices measurably change accuracy.
- **Contamination awareness**: GPT-3's authors had to check test data wasn't
  in training — the ancestor of every leakage audit since.

## Why learn this

This paper teaches you to think in interfaces: training is not the only way
to add capability; examples at inference time are a substitute with their
own scaling curve. And its contamination analysis is a masterclass in
intellectual honesty — distrusting your own best numbers is a transferable
research skill.
